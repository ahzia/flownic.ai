# **1\. Executive summary**

| Product definition: Flownic is a practice network for high-stakes spoken assessments. It matches candidates for structured mock sessions while AI runs the format, guides the person acting as examiner, and turns each session into evidence-based feedback and a next-practice plan. |
| :---- |

The launch product should remain deliberately narrow: one German B1 speaking-exam format, one geographic/community wedge, and one behavior to prove—strangers will schedule, complete, and repeat a structured peer mock session. The future platform can support many languages, exam types, professional oral assessments, and selected interviews, but that breadth should be built through reusable exam blueprints rather than a generic “practice anything” interface.

Flownic’s central product insight is not that peer matching, role switching, AI scoring, or AI interviewers are individually new. They are not. Exponent already combines peer and AI interview practice; HelloTalk offers timed reciprocal language exchanges; Educato advertises live peer simulations; VivaPair matches medical trainees for structured oral-exam practice; and multiple products provide AI-only exam simulations. Flownic’s opportunity is the specific combination of exam-format orchestration, examiner-only AI assistance, role switching, reliability-aware scheduling, calibrated feedback, and an immediate AI fallback for underserved spoken assessments. \[S1–S14\]

## **Strategic decision**

* **Launch wedge.** Start with one German B1 speaking-exam track. Telc Deutsch B1 is the recommended first content blueprint because it includes interaction and planning tasks that expose the value of a real partner; if pilot recruitment is materially stronger for Goethe B1 or DTZ, choose that single format instead.

* **Core mode.** Peer practice is the default and creates the differentiated experience. AI is the examiner-copilot, quality layer, and guaranteed fallback—not merely a chatbot that replaces the human.

* **Commercial model.** Use short-duration exam passes, a paid expert marketplace, and cohort licenses. Exam preparation is urgent but temporary, so 30- and 90-day products fit better than relying only on annual consumer subscriptions.

* **Expansion rule.** Add an exam, language, or interview category only after its content blueprint, rubric, distribution channel, and supply/liquidity plan are ready.

# **2\. Product foundation**

## **Mission, category, and promise**

**Mission.** Make realistic, repeatable preparation for high-stakes spoken assessments affordable and accessible to anyone, regardless of location, network, or ability to pay for frequent private coaching.

**Category.** AI-guided peer practice for high-stakes spoken assessments.

**Customer promise.** Practice the conversation before it counts.

Long-term vision. A candidate should be able to select an exam, certification, interview, or oral assessment anywhere in the world and immediately receive the right practice mode: a compatible peer, a verified specialist, or an AI examiner using the same structured blueprint.

## **The problem Flownic solves**

Candidates can usually find official sample tasks, textbooks, videos, vocabulary apps, and generic AI chatbots. The unresolved gap is repeated performance under the social and procedural conditions of the real event: another person waiting, turn-taking, interruptions, unfamiliar accents, timed tasks, follow-up questions, and uncertainty about whether an answer met the rubric.

* **Finding the right partner is operational work.** Candidates search informal groups, compare levels, negotiate a time, share materials, and manage no-shows.

* **Unstructured conversation is not a mock exam.** General exchange improves fluency but may not reproduce task order, timing, roles, or assessment criteria.

* **AI-only practice is convenient but socially incomplete.** It reduces scheduling friction, yet often removes the human pressure, negotiation, interruption, and behavioral variability that candidates need to rehearse.

* **Teachers are valuable but expensive for high-frequency repetition.** Expert time should be used where judgment matters most, not for every routine practice repetition.

* **Feedback is either absent or overconfident.** A useful product must show evidence, uncertainty, and the specific next behavior to practice rather than presenting an AI estimate as an official score.

## **Product principles**

* **Human where humans matter.** Use peers for social pressure, interaction, turn-taking, and unpredictability.

* **AI where AI creates access.** Use AI for structure, immediate availability, consistency, translation, and affordable feedback.

* **Practice, not certification.** Never imply official affiliation, official questions, guaranteed results, or authoritative scores.

* **One role, one screen.** The acting examiner receives guidance; the candidate receives no hidden help during the mock.

* **Evidence before score.** Feedback cites transcript moments, rubric criteria, and confidence limits.

* **Urgency-native.** The exam date, target score, and available practice time drive matching and the plan.

* **Safety by default.** Consent, reporting, privacy, age policy, accessibility, and no-show protection are product features.

* **Blueprint, not hard-coded course.** Every exam is represented by a versioned interaction blueprint that can be validated and updated.

# **3\. Market, customers, and expansion logic**

## **Why this can become a large category**

The initial German B1 segment is a wedge, not the entire market. The broader demand consists of candidates whose education, migration, employment, professional license, or career progression depends on a spoken performance. IELTS alone reports more than four million test takers annually; Goethe materials state that more than half a million people take its German examinations in a typical year; telc lists more than 3,000 examination centres and exams across all 24 official EU languages and more. These figures do not equal Flownic’s addressable customers, but they demonstrate a recurring global pipeline of time-bound, high-stakes learners. \[S15–S18\]

The most attractive tracks are not necessarily the largest. A smaller exam can be commercially better when speaking is hard to practise alone, candidates cluster in reachable communities, the rubric is clear, exam dates create urgency, and existing online preparation is weak.

## **Select expansion tracks by evidence, not enthusiasm**

Score every proposed track from 1–5 on the following criteria before allocating content or engineering capacity:

* Stakes and deadline urgency.

* Speaking interaction dependency: how much does a real human improve realism?

* Reachable candidate density in one geography or online community.

* Clarity and stability of public rubric, timing, and task format.

* Existing practice gap and competitor intensity.

* Ability to create original, legally safe practice content.

* Expected willingness to pay and expert-session value.

* Reuse of the existing session, matching, and feedback infrastructure.

* Availability of teachers or specialists for calibration and marketplace supply.

## **The open product gap**

* **Exam-specific liquidity.** Matching is not by language alone; it uses exam, level, exam date, format, availability, timezone, and reliability.

* **AI-enabled human examiner.** A normal candidate can conduct a consistent mock because AI privately manages instructions, timing, probes, and the rubric.

* **Reciprocal learning.** Role switching lets each participant practise production and learn by evaluating another response.

* **Guaranteed availability.** If matching fails or a peer does not attend, the same blueprint switches to an AI examiner without losing the session.

* **Quality ladder.** The same flow supports a free peer, verified high-rated peer, teacher, exam specialist, or AI examiner.

* **Evidence-linked progress.** Reports combine AI observations, structured peer feedback, optional expert review, and longitudinal trends.

* **Institutional private pools.** Schools can assign practice inside their cohort, then use the public network or AI to fill gaps.

## **Claims Flownic should not make**

* “No one combines peers and AI.” Exponent and Educato already provide meaningful precedents.

* “Language exchange is always unstructured.” HelloTalk now offers timed reciprocal calls; the difference is exam fidelity and assessment.

* “Our AI score is the same as an official score.” Use “practice estimate,” show confidence, and validate against experts.

* “We use real exam questions.” Use original exam-style content unless licensed; respect trademarks and official materials.

* “More languages means a bigger moat.” Breadth without content quality, liquidity, or distribution increases cost and confusion.

# **5\. The future-state Flownic platform**

## **End-to-end customer journey**

1. **Define the goal.** Select the exact exam/interview, date, current level, target, preferred practice language, feedback language, timezone, and accessibility needs.

2. **Establish a baseline.** Complete a short diagnostic or import recent results; identify the two or three highest-impact weaknesses.

3. **Choose a practice mode.** Peer, AI examiner, verified peer, teacher/exam specialist, recurring partner, or private cohort.

4. **Match and schedule.** Use exam fit, date proximity, reliability, availability, and preferences; provide reminders, waitlist replacement, and an AI fallback.

5. **Run the structured session.** The session blueprint controls instructions, preparation time, tasks, role visibility, follow-ups, recording consent, and role switching.

6. **Receive evidence-based feedback.** Show rubric-aligned strengths, transcript/audio evidence, corrections, estimated readiness with uncertainty, and peer comments.

7. **Act on the next step.** Generate a focused practice plan, drills, mistake cards, a recommended next session, and an optional expert review.

## **Feature portfolio**

### **A. Discovery and onboarding**

* Exam and level catalog with clear independent-product disclaimers.

* Exam-date countdown, target outcome, and weekly practice availability.

* Native language and feedback-language selection independent from the target language.

* Baseline diagnostic; optional import of a prior practice result.

* Goal-based plan that recommends repetitions, not an oversized generic curriculum.

* Low-bandwidth, audio-only, caption, keyboard, and accessibility preferences.

### **B. Matching, scheduling, and liquidity**

* Match on exact exam blueprint, level band, exam-date proximity, timezone, availability, and reliability.

* Offer fixed high-density slots before fully flexible matching; concentrate supply instead of fragmenting it.

* Calendar invitations, reminders, confirmation checkpoints, waiting room, late grace period, and one-click reschedule.

* Replacement queue and automatic AI fallback when a peer cancels or does not attend.

* Recurring partner, “practice again,” invite-a-friend, and school/private cohort pools.

* Reliability score based primarily on behavior—attendance, completion, and helpfulness—not popularity.

### **C. Live session engine**

* Role-specific lobby, preparation, examiner, candidate, observer, and assessor states.

* Exam blueprint with stage order, timer, task card, permitted preparation, transition rules, and role switching.

* Examiner-only AI suggestions: follow-up, clarification, pacing, fairness reminder, and coverage check.

* Candidate view intentionally excludes answer suggestions during the mock.

* Video, audio-only, captions, connection-quality fallback, and synchronized timer.

* Consent-aware recording, transcript, bookmarks, and “flag this moment” controls.

* In-session safety tools: mute, leave, report, block, and moderator escalation.

### **D. Feedback and longitudinal progress**

* Rubric-aligned dimensions appropriate to the specific track: task completion, fluency, coherence, grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation, interaction, professional judgment, or structured thinking.

* Evidence snippets linked to transcript timestamps and, when consented, short audio moments.

* Confidence range and calibration note; never present a single AI score as official truth.

* Structured peer feedback with constrained prompts to reduce vague or harmful comments.

* Optional expert review that can correct the AI report and feed calibration data.

* Mistake notebook, saved vocabulary/phrases, personalized drills, and next-session objectives.

* Progress trends across peer, AI, and expert sessions, with comparable blueprints clearly labeled.

### **E. Content and platform operations**

* Versioned exam blueprints separated from questions, rubrics, timing rules, and localization.

* Original question authoring with human review, difficulty tags, cultural and safety review, and retirement dates.

* Expert calibration sets with anonymized responses and expected rating bands.

* Admin dashboard for sessions, no-shows, disputes, content versions, reports, refunds, and AI-cost monitoring.

* Teacher dashboard for assignments, cohort completion, common weaknesses, and privacy-safe progress.

* Creator/expert portal for approved content contributions and paid sessions, with audit trails.

## **Multilingual product requirements**

Supporting a language is more than translating the interface. Flownic must independently model the target language, the language of instructions, the language of feedback, locale-specific examples, right-to-left layout, speech-recognition quality, voice quality, rubric terminology, and moderation capability.

* Separate target-language content from feedback-language explanations.

* Localize time, calendar, payment, names, examples, and accessibility—not only labels.

* Measure transcription and pronunciation performance across accents and devices before launch.

* Maintain a human reviewer and moderation path for every supported language.

* Publish known limitations when the AI is less reliable for a language, accent, or noisy environment.

* Do not launch a low-liquidity language without fixed slots, a partner, or an AI-first fallback.

## **Content governance and intellectual property**

* Create original exam-style tasks; use official sample material only where terms permit or a license exists.

* Describe compatibility carefully—e.g., “practice for telc-style B1 speaking”—and state that Flownic is independent and not endorsed by exam owners.

* Version blueprints when an exam changes; record which version each session used.

* Use qualified reviewers and documented rubrics; avoid opaque prompt-only content generation.

* Retire leaked, duplicated, culturally problematic, or low-performing prompts.

# **7\. Interview preparation**

## **Why interviews are a credible—but later—extension**

Interview practice uses the same core infrastructure: a target role, a structured question blueprint, interviewer-only prompts, dynamic follow-ups, time control, transcript evidence, feedback, scheduling, and a quality ladder from peer to expert. However, the general interview market is crowded. Flownic should not lead with “AI mock interviews” or “Pramp plus AI.” Its strongest bridge is high-stakes interviews conducted in a non-native language, followed by selected role categories where peer interaction adds value.

| Recommended bridge: Start with German-language job interviews for international professionals already moving through Flownic’s German exam funnel. This extends the customer journey without abandoning the language-and-integration distribution advantage. |
| :---- |

## **Interview product configuration**

* **Inputs.** CV/resume, job description, company, role, seniority, interview stage, language, target competencies, and optional recruiter guidance.

* **Modes.** AI interviewer, reciprocal peer mock, verified peer in the same function, coach/recruiter, or panel simulation.

* **Tracks.** Behavioral/STAR, hiring-manager screen, role knowledge, product sense, system design, coding discussion, data, sales discovery, case interview, and admissions.

* **Feedback.** Relevance, structure, evidence, clarity, concision, confidence, language quality, follow-up handling, and role-specific reasoning.

* **Artifacts.** Answer library, evidence/story bank, missing-experience map, improved answer outline, and a targeted second mock.

## **Interview-specific differentiation**

* Peer matches can reflect role, seniority, interview date, company stage, and reliability—not only a generic question category.

* The acting interviewer receives AI guidance and rubric reminders, while the candidate receives no answer support.

* Practice can happen in the target working language with explanations in the user’s strongest language.

* An expert can review only the high-value moments rather than requiring a full coaching hour.

* The platform can compare performance across AI, peer, and expert modes over several sessions.

## **Ethical boundary: practice only**

| Product policy: Flownic should not provide hidden answers, stealth overlays, or real-time assistance during an actual job interview, exam, or assessment. The product should help users prepare before the event and reflect afterward. This protects trust, partnerships, and the long-term brand. |
| :---- |

This boundary also sharpens positioning against products that advertise discreet live-interview assistance. Flownic is a preparation and coaching network, not a cheating tool.

## **Marketplace economics**

* Suggested take rate: 20% at launch, tested against supply acquisition and payment/support cost.

* Allow specialists to set a price inside approved bands; show credentials, languages, track experience, reviews, and completed sessions.

* Use a transparent cancellation and no-show policy with partial or full credits depending on who cancels and when.

* Do not subsidize every expert session indefinitely; targeted launch credits should have a measured acquisition purpose.

* Separate expert earnings from AI-score validation incentives to avoid conflicts of interest.

## **Unit-economic guardrails**

* **Consumer gross margin.** Target at least 75% before paid acquisition by controlling transcription, model, storage, and video costs.

* **AI fair use.** Package a predictable number of full mocks; allow cheaper drills more freely; show usage clearly.

* **Media efficiency.** Prefer audio as the default when video does not improve the exam simulation; retain recordings only as long as users choose.

* **Marketplace contribution.** Measure take-rate revenue after payment, refund, support, and specialist-acquisition costs.

* **Cohort profitability.** Price onboarding, support, AI allowance, and instructor analytics explicitly; do not sell unlimited usage before observing it.

## **Pricing experiments**

* Test €9.99, €14.99, and €19.99 30-day passes on equivalent user cohorts.

* Compare a time pass with credit bundles; measure clarity, completion, and breakage—not only conversion.

* Offer the paid upgrade immediately after a useful free report, not before the user understands value.

* Test whether priority matching, more AI practice, or detailed progress is the strongest paid trigger.

* For schools, compare a fixed cohort pilot with active-seat pricing and document staff time saved.

# **9\. Go-to-market and network liquidity**

## **Cold-start strategy**

Flownic is a marketplace-like product whose first failure mode is empty or unreliable sessions. The launch should concentrate users by exam, date range, and time slot. Fully flexible global matching sounds convenient but fragments scarce supply.

* **Start with scheduled rooms.** Publish a small number of high-density recurring slots, such as weekday evenings and weekend mornings.

* **Run concierge matching.** Manually recruit, pair, remind, replace, and interview participants until behavior is understood.

* **Seed both roles.** Teachers, alumni, and founder-led hosts can ensure early sessions have competent examiners.

* **Guarantee the session.** If a peer is absent, offer an AI exam immediately or a replacement credit.

* **Reward reliability.** Give earlier access and better matches to users who attend, complete both roles, and provide useful feedback.

* **Create recurring cohorts.** Group users by exam month so the same people can repeat without rebuilding trust each time.

## **Initial acquisition channels**

***Table 11\. Early distribution should optimize completed sessions, not raw registrations.***

| Channel | Offer | Why it can work | Metric |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Language schools / teachers | Free pilot slot or private cohort | Concentrated candidates, trusted distribution, expert feedback. | Activated learners per partner; completed sessions. |
| German exam communities | Exam-specific free mock day | High intent in WhatsApp, Telegram, Facebook, Reddit, and local groups. | Qualified signup to first-session conversion. |
| Search and content | Exam structure guides, speaking checklists, mock clips | Users search by exact exam and date; content compounds. | Organic activation and paid conversion. |
| Invite loop | Bring a known partner; Flownic provides structure and feedback | Removes the cold-start dependency while exposing product value. | Invites sent, accepted, and completed. |
| Ambassadors / alumni | Host practice rooms and earn specialist status | Creates trustworthy supply and community leadership. | Sessions hosted; show rate; repeat users. |
| Workforce / integration programs | Cohort practice and progress visibility | Outcome-aligned institutions can fund access. | Pilot renewal and learner completion. |

## **Growth loop**

1. A candidate joins for a free exam-specific session.

2. The platform needs a second participant, so the user invites a classmate or enters a fixed slot.

3. Both complete two roles and receive a useful report.

4. The report recommends a focused second session and makes it easy to rebook the same partner or a better match.

5. Reliable users improve network quality; successful candidates can return as verified peers or ambassadors.

6. Schools and experts see aggregated demand and contribute supply, content, and credibility.

## **Geographic sequencing**

Begin where the founders have direct access: Germany, with concentrated recruitment in NRW and online German-learning communities. Expand nationally through exam-specific online cohorts before adding another country. Geography matters less for video delivery than for distribution, trust, payment, moderation, and institutional partnerships.

# **10\. Trust, safety, quality, and platform operations**

## **Safety and community standards**

* 18+ MVP unless a dedicated minor-safety design and institutional guardian flow are implemented.

* Explicit consent before recording or transcription; show who can access each artifact.

* One-click block/report/leave; reasons for harassment, cheating requests, hate, sexual conduct, fraud, impersonation, and unsafe content.

* Moderation playbook with response times, appeal path, repeat-offender policy, and emergency escalation boundaries.

* Optional preference controls where lawful and justified, while avoiding discriminatory matching defaults.

* Reliability score separated from language ability so weaker learners are not punished for being learners.

## **AI feedback quality**

* Benchmark against multiple qualified raters, not one teacher, and measure inter-rater disagreement.

* Calibrate per exam, level, task type, language, accent range, and recording condition.

* Show evidence and confidence; allow users and experts to challenge a feedback item.

* Track systematic error by accent, first language, gendered voice characteristics, disability, and device quality where ethically and legally appropriate.

* Use human-reviewed anchor responses and regression tests whenever a model or prompt changes.

* Keep practice feedback distinct from any formal admission, hiring, licensing, or certification decision.

## **Privacy and data principles**

* Collect the minimum data required for matching, practice, feedback, safety, and payment.

* Provide retention choices for audio/video; allow users to delete recordings and accounts, subject to lawful safety/payment retention.

* Separate operational data from model-improvement consent; do not assume recordings may train models.

* Encrypt data in transit and at rest, restrict staff access, log sensitive access, and execute processor agreements.

* For institutions, define controller/processor roles, data visibility, exports, and deletion contractually.

* Obtain specialist legal review before expanding into minors, official assessment, or employer-facing evaluation.

5. **Feedback calibration.** Have qualified raters independently score anonymized sessions and quantify agreement with AI dimensions.

1. Confirm user segment, exam version, and distribution partner.

2. Create and review the interaction blueprint and original tasks.

3. Run sessions manually and identify where users or acting examiners fail.

5. Calibrate feedback against qualified reviewers.

# **13\. MVP definition and guardrails**

| MVP objective: Prove that German B1 candidates will reliably complete and repeat an AI-guided peer mock speaking session, and that the feedback is useful enough to support a paid short-duration preparation product. |
| :---- |

## **Recommended first content decision**

Use one exact exam blueprint. Telc Deutsch B1 is the working recommendation because its oral format contains interaction and planning behavior that makes a peer materially useful. If the first recruitable cohort is stronger for Goethe B1 or DTZ, switch the blueprint before launch—but do not combine multiple formats into a vague “German B1” mock.

## **MVP in scope**

* Authentication and a simple exam profile: exact exam, date, level, timezone, availability, and feedback language.

* Founder-managed fixed slots plus basic availability matching.

* Confirmation, reminder, reschedule, waiting room, and no-show state.

* Browser audio/video session or a stable external-call fallback during concierge testing.

* Separate examiner and candidate screens; AI suggestions visible only to the examiner.

* One validated exam blueprint with preparation time, stages, timer, task cards, and role switching.

* Live or near-live transcription and a concise post-session feedback report.

* AI examiner fallback for unmatched, immediate, or failed peer sessions.

* Basic attendance/helpfulness rating, report/block controls, and an admin operations page.

* Analytics for booking, confirmation, attendance, completion, role switch, report open, repeat booking, and payment.

* One payment test: 30-day Exam Sprint or paid expert report review.

## **Explicitly out of MVP**

* Multiple exam formats or multiple target languages.

* Native iOS/Android apps.

* A full reading/listening/writing curriculum or a large question bank.

* Open-ended social feed, group chat, gamified currency, or broad language exchange.

* Self-serve teacher marketplace with complex payouts.

* Institution SSO, white-labeling, advanced cohort analytics, or enterprise integrations.

* Detailed word-level pronunciation diagnostics unless already reliable at low cost.

* Official-score claims, pass guarantees, or certification.

* General job-interview product or live assistance during real interviews.

* Automated matching sophistication that replaces useful manual operations before demand exists.

## **MVP critical flow**

1. **Book.** Candidate selects the exact exam and one of a small number of available practice slots.

2. **Confirm.** Both users confirm; reminders and a replacement queue reduce no-shows.

3. **Prepare.** Users see role instructions and consent; the examiner receives the task privately.

4. **Practise.** The acting examiner follows AI timing and probes; the candidate responds without assistance.

5. **Switch.** The system resets role state and runs the reciprocal round.

6. **Review.** Both receive a short evidence-linked report and structured peer feedback.

7. **Repeat.** The product recommends the next focus and offers one-click rebooking or an AI fallback.

## **MVP acceptance criteria**

***Table 15\. The MVP is a business experiment, not a feature checklist.***

| Criterion | Definition of done |
| :---- | :---- |
| Session completion | A new user can book, join, finish both roles, and open a report without founder intervention in the normal path. |
| Role privacy | Candidate never sees examiner questions, follow-ups, or evaluation guidance before/during the round. |
| Fallback | If a peer does not attend, the user can start an AI version within the same scheduled window. |
| Quality | Qualified reviewers judge the blueprint as faithful and the report as useful, with limitations clearly stated. |
| Safety | Consent, leave, block, report, and retention controls are visible and functional. |
| Measurement | Every funnel step and failure state is logged with an agreed definition. |
| Commercial | At least one user or institution pays for a defined product—not a donation or unrelated service. |

# **14\. Key risks and mitigations**

***Table 16\. Risk register for product and business execution.***

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation | Early warning |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |

# **15\. Decisions, assumptions, and open questions**

## **Decisions this overview recommends locking**

* Flownic is a practice network for high-stakes spoken assessments, not a general language-learning app.

* The first launch uses one exact German B1 oral-exam blueprint.

* Peer practice is the differentiated core; AI is the copilot, quality layer, and fallback.

* Feedback is a practice estimate with evidence and uncertainty, never an official score.

* Consumer monetization uses short exam/interview passes rather than only annual subscriptions.

* Verified human sessions and institution cohorts are distinct paid layers.

* Interview assistance is limited to preparation; no hidden live-event copilot.

* Expansion requires a track owner, qualified reviewer, content policy, distribution plan, and liquidity strategy.

## **Assumptions to validate immediately**

* Which exact B1 exam provides the densest reachable first cohort: telc, Goethe, or DTZ?

* Will candidates accept reciprocal role switching, or do they prefer one-sided candidate practice?

* What fixed slots produce the highest show and completion rates?

* Does AI fallback rescue a failed booking without materially reducing satisfaction?

* Which report element drives another session: estimated readiness, evidence, corrections, drills, or expert review?

* Will users pay €14.99 for a 30-day pass, and at what moment in the journey?

* Can teachers review/calibrate sessions quickly enough to make feedback trustworthy and economically viable?

* Do schools buy for speaking repetitions, instructor analytics, learner confidence, or exam outcomes?
